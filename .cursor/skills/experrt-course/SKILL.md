# Experrt courses

A course is worth paying for when a professional can finish it and do the work on Monday, and can say why. The first prompt course failed that test. It was four short captions, a check labelled Holds and Invented, and a purchase that opened the lesson with no way to come back and sign in. Use this skill before writing or changing a lesson.

## Voice

Write the way a good teacher talks to a colleague.

- Every sentence is a full sentence. A paragraph can be read aloud.
- Teach the idea before you test it. Say what it is, say what it is not, then show it on a real piece of work.
- The words on a button are words the lesson has already explained.
- The reader is a professional. Address them as you. Do not quiz them on our private vocabulary.
- Keep published salary figures on the sales page, with the source. A lesson does not promise that pay will change.
- The signed record names the person and the work they produced. It does not claim compliance with any regulation.

## Shape of a course

One course, one outcome. Prompt Engineering for Professional Work has this outcome: the learner can explain what a prompt is, tell a sentence that only thanks someone from a sentence that commits the company, and write a prompt a colleague can run without asking what was meant.

A lesson teaches one move. Use this order, and do not skip to the check.

1. Place. One or two sentences on where this sits in the course.
2. Idea. The definition, in full sentences.
3. Worked example. Show the prompt, the output, and a reading of what happened.
4. Practice. The learner does that same move. The controls use the words just taught.
5. Check. A new case, the same move. The question is a full sentence and says what to do.
6. Bridge. One sentence on what the next lesson adds.

## Checks

The check is where the current course loses the learner. Build the next ones to these rules.

- State the task in a full sentence. "The prompt was only: write a polite reply. Mark each sentence." does not say what the buttons mean.
- Define the labels in the lesson, then repeat that meaning on the button. For the reply exercise the buttons are "Only thanks them" and "Adds a promise that was not in the prompt". Holds and Invented are not taught, so they are not labels.
- Keep Continue disabled until the learner has answered every part.
- A wrong answer stays on the lesson. The feedback names the sentence or the missing part, in a full sentence, and says what to look at again.
- A right answer shows why it is right. The learner then chooses Next. Do not jump to the next lesson before they have read that.
- A build task looks for the parts of the work: a role, a fact that is true, a limit on what must not be invented, and the shape of the answer. A character count alone is not a pass.
- An ordering task is only for a skill where the order matters. Do not ask someone to sort steps that the instruction has already explained.

## The screen

- The catalogue and the sales page use the site header.
- The lesson player has one bar: back to the course, and Lessons. Do not put the site header (AI LMS, Get Started) above the lesson bar. Two headers hid the question on a phone.
- The question sits fully above the choices. On a phone the learner can read the whole task without the choices covering it.
- One task at a time. Controls are large enough to tap.

## Coming back

Payment today stores a cookie in the browser that paid. That is why a buyer cannot sign up and open the course later on another device.

The next account work, and not a separate registration before payment:

- Stripe already collects the email. That email is the account email.
- After payment, on the way into lesson one, ask them to save a password or send a sign-in link to that email.
- Attach the purchase to that user. Progress and the prompt card stay on the purchase.
- The current browser keeps working from the cookie. Sign in is how a second device opens the same course.
- The receipt still thanks them and links them back. The alert still goes to ag@experrt.com.

## Prompt Engineering for Professional Work

Rewrite this course to the six lessons below. Do not ship another version that is four captions and a jargon check. Leave the other courses as outlines until this one is something a person would pay for.

1. What a prompt is. A prompt is the instruction and the evidence you give a model before it writes. It is the only information the model has about this client, this price, and this deadline.
2. What happens when the prompt is silent. The model still writes. The gap is filled with something that sounds finished. Work the "write a polite reply" example here, including why a thank-you can stand and a discount cannot.
3. The parts of a prompt, one at a time. Who is speaking. The facts that are true. What must not be added or promised. The shape of the answer. Each part gets a before and an after, not a list of four nouns.
4. Read a reply. Mark each sentence with the words from lesson 2. The learner can say which sentence they would send and which sentence commits the company.
5. Repair the prompt. The learner edits the same prompt: read the sentence that went too far, name the missing limit, add it, and read the new reply. They do the edit. They do not reorder a list of instructions.
6. Write a prompt for a real task. The learner produces the prompt card. The check accepts it when a colleague could run it. They sign the record.

Each lesson is long enough to teach the move: several paragraphs, one worked example, one practice, one check. A person who finishes can brief a model on a real piece of work and can explain why a sentence is safe to send.

## Leave alone until this course is right

Do not write lessons for the other thirty-nine courses. Do not add points, streaks, or a second visual system. Do not turn on Stripe Tax. The sales page stays the paper brand. The lesson stays a quiet page for reading and doing the work.
