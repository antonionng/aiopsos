# Experrt: an agentic learning platform for providers and enterprises

Strategy revision | 9 September 2026

## The decision

Build a full learning platform whose agents help people operate learning programmes. Sell it to training providers and enterprise L&D teams. Use your own training company as the first operating customer, with the same product boundaries that external customers will use.

This replaces the previous proposal's academy-first commercial positioning. Its findings about contradictory copy, weak metrics and fragmented journeys remain relevant. The ambition is now larger: dependable LMS and training-business operations, with agents coordinating the work.

Working category: **Agentic learning platform**.

Plain-language description: **Run training, guide learning and keep a reliable record of progress.**

AI and robotics are the initial curriculum specialisms and a demanding test of practical learning. The core platform should accommodate other subjects so external providers can bring their own expertise. Agentic describes how the product works; AI and robotics describe where your own academy initially specialises.

All capabilities described below are proposed unless explicitly identified as present in the reviewed source. Market references describe vendor publications, not independently verified product performance.

## 1. One product, three operating contexts

| Context | Primary job | Success looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Your training company | Sell, prepare and deliver programmes with less manual coordination | Every live engagement runs through the platform; clients receive useful records |
| Other training providers | Operate their own branded learning business across multiple clients | Repeatable delivery, protected intellectual property, clear client reporting and viable margins |
| Enterprise L&D | Plan and deliver learning across employees, internal experts and external providers | Skills priorities become funded programmes with accountable delivery and evidence |

Providers and enterprises should be capabilities an organisation can possess, rather than permanently exclusive organisation types. An enterprise can deliver its own courses. A provider can train its own staff. Both can buy learning from another provider.

Your academy is a normal provider workspace. Platform administration is a separate context. Routine delivery must not depend on super-admin access, direct database changes or private founder-only workflows.

There is also a commercial trust issue: another training company may regard your academy as a competitor. They need private client lists, protected content, independent branding, explicit support access and no automatic promotion of your courses to their customers. Enforce those boundaries in the product.

## 3. Product design: objectives, work and records

The opening experience should be a workspace showing active programmes and decisions. Users can express an objective naturally, but the result becomes a structured plan they can inspect and edit.

Example objective: “Prepare the customer support team to use our approved AI tools next month, using our internal material and two facilitated sessions.”

The platform produces an audience, proposed outcomes, relevant source material, pathway, schedule, resource requirements and unresolved questions. It shows which facts it retrieved and which choices it inferred. The owner edits the plan and authorises the relevant operations.

The core visible objects are **Programme, People, Learning, Work and Records**. Commercial provider tools sit within the relevant programme or client context. Calendar, table, editor and report views remain available because they are efficient for reviewing structured information.

One primary agent interface coordinates the experience. Users should not have to choose between ten named agents before starting a task. Specialist capabilities can operate behind the interface, with a shared task record.

### The five principal screens

1. **Today:** what is running, what changed, what needs attention, and what the platform has completed. Show only actionable exceptions and useful summaries.
2. **Programme workspace:** goal, audience, design, delivery, practice, evidence and commercial details, filtered by role. A timeline connects these views.
3. **Learning studio:** source material, outline, activities, practice, assessment, feedback and publication. Each course version has an owner and review state.
4. **Learner workspace:** next action, current task, session, coach and feedback. The coach helps the learner think, practise and improve.
5. **Records and reports:** searchable, exportable facts with provenance, corrections and permissions. Users can inspect records without asking an agent to interpret them.

The important interaction change is that the platform prepares and carries out work within authorised boundaries. Its tables and forms remain dependable ways to verify and correct that work.

## 4. Two complete workflows

### Provider: client request to completed programme

Illustrative request: “Create a six-week AI programme for this client, using our approved course material, three groups and a fixed delivery budget.”

The platform retrieves the client's agreed requirements and licensed materials, drafts the programme, checks prerequisites and identifies trainer/resource conflicts. Missing availability is shown as unknown. It prepares a costed proposal and a list of decisions.

After the owner authorises the commercial offer, the programme tracks acceptance and becomes schedulable delivery. Accepted scope is versioned. Enrolment links, preparation and reminders follow the provider's configured rules. Changes to price, agreed scope or client-facing commitments return to the authorised owner.

During delivery, facilitators record attendance and review work. The platform gathers missing records, prepares progress summaries and identifies blocked learners without inventing causes. Completion rules run against authoritative results. Eligible certificates can be issued automatically under an approved issuance policy; disputed or practical assessor decisions remain human-owned.

At close, the provider has delivery results and a margin view; the client receives its agreed report; the learner receives their personal record. Agents prepare renewal recommendations from declared review dates and customer needs, rather than using private learner conversation content.

### Enterprise: business change to maintained capability

Illustrative request: “We are introducing a new robotic workcell at two sites. Prepare the affected teams before launch.”

An authorised source identifies the relevant roles, sites and task requirements. The platform compares those requirements with existing scoped evidence, identifies gaps and drafts a programme using internal and contracted provision.

L&D reviews the population, target skills, budget and delivery plan. Operations approves the work-specific requirements. The platform coordinates approved assignments and available sessions, while routing missing capacity or conflicting dates to the responsible person.

Learners complete preparation and practice. A qualified assessor records practical results against the relevant equipment and task. Employer authorisation is a separate decision with its own scope. L&D receives progress and operations receives evidence coverage for the required tasks.

When approved operating instructions change, the system identifies potentially affected learning and evidence. The content or operational owner decides the impact before refresher assignments are created. A changed document does not automatically invalidate everyone's qualification.

## 5. What agentic must mean in the product

An agent has a goal, scoped authority, access to authorised records, allowed actions, a stopping condition and an execution history. It can respond to an event, plan work, take permitted actions, inspect the result and route exceptions. A generated recommendation alone does not meet this definition.

| Capability | Trigger | Useful output or action | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Programme planning | Approved brief or new learning need | Draft pathway, audience, resources and schedule | Owner accepts the plan and commercial assumptions |
| Learning design | Approved source material | Draft activities, practice and assessment with source references | Expert publishes content and formal rubrics |
| Delivery coordination | Programme authorised, session changes, missing information | Prepare groups, reconcile schedules, run configured reminders | New costs and changed external commitments need authority |
| Learner coaching | Learner request or agreed checkpoint | Hints, retrieval practice, explanations and feedback for practice | Formal results follow approved assessment rules; no fabricated evidence |
| Evidence preparation | Results released or reporting period closes | Assemble records, find omissions and draft a report | No invented attendance, grades or competence |
| Content maintenance | Approved source version changes | Impact list and proposed revisions | Content owner decides relevance and publishes |

Begin with programme preparation and delivery coordination. Those workflows are close to the existing code, repeated in your business and measurable. Add broader autonomous operation after their failure cases are understood.

Autonomy is configured by action: read; draft; execute after confirmation; execute within a standing policy; or human-only. A reminder under an approved schedule should not require a fresh click every time. A new contract, refund, published curriculum revision or equipment authorisation has a different authority requirement.

Users need to see the scope, preview changes, inspect completed actions, stop a run and recover from failure. An agent that cannot complete a step must report partial completion precisely.

## 6. Records: the durable core

Use authoritative records and explicit state transitions for facts. Keep generated explanations and inferred skill suggestions separate. A model response never becomes a grade, invoice, enrolment or authorisation simply because it says one exists.

| Record family | Principal records | Authority and important distinctions |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and access | Person, organisation, membership, role, provider-client relationship | Memberships are scoped and time-bound; shared email/domain does not grant access |
| Curriculum | Course, immutable published version, activity, source, rubric, licence | Provider or enterprise content owner publishes; assignments reference a defined version |
| Programme and delivery | Brief, programme, assignment, cohort, session, attendance, resource booking | Delivery owner controls events; attendees' employers access agreed slices |
| Learning evidence | Attempt, submission, assessment result, observation, credential, correction | Keep self-report, automatic scoring, human review and authorisation distinct |
| Commercial operations | Client, offer, accepted order, invoice, payment reference, entitlement | Payment processor confirms settlement; accounting integration defines ledger authority |
| Agent execution | Goal, policy, plan, action, approval, external result, error | Every action identifies actor, workspace, scope and resulting record |

Model the relationships explicitly: a programme uses course versions; assignments connect people to the programme; attempts produce evidence; reviewed evidence supports skills claims; credentials apply defined completion rules. Workplace authorisation is an additional record, not a renamed certificate.

A skill claim needs the skill definition and version, person, evidence type, task context, assessor or scoring method, date and review conditions. Model-inferred skills stay suggestions until supported by the required evidence.

Correct mistakes through traceable revisions or superseding records. Preserve frozen reports as the snapshot they were when issued, with later corrections linked. Record retention and deletion rules still apply; auditability does not imply keeping every personal detail forever.

An xAPI learning record store is useful for interoperable activity evidence. It does not replace programme, commercial, identity and authorisation records. ADL describes xAPI/LRS as infrastructure for communicating and recording learning activity. Source: [ADL learning activity guidance](https://www.adlnet.gov/guides/tla/service-definitions/xAPI-Adoption-TLA-CMM-Level-1.html).

## 7. Provider-client relationships are a first-class feature

Avoid treating every enterprise customer as a child account wholly owned by a provider. A customer may use several providers and run internal training. Providers may serve many customers and have independent content rights.

Create an explicit delivery agreement connecting provider and enterprise. It defines the programme, participant scope, reporting fields, content entitlement and operational permissions. Branding does not establish permission.

For a shared class, the provider and assigned facilitator can manage its authorised roster. Each enterprise sees its own participants and the agreed session information. Learners see their own records and shared teaching material. Provider financial margins, other client rosters and private coaching are not included in the client's report by default.

A learner can have several legitimate memberships. Changing employer must not automatically expose the old employer's material or move all historical records into the new organisation. Personal credential sharing is explicit, with provenance and revocation status retained.

Your existing cross-organisation cohort model already distinguishes delivering organisation from attending employer. That is a valuable foundation. The reviewed profile model still centres identity on one organisation and role; full provider-client operation needs richer memberships, agreements and scoped authorisation. ## 8. A credible LMS foundation

The existing README deliberately excludes content hosting and SCORM playback. That boundary needs revising for this strategy. The existing material and submission workspace is useful, but it is not a complete self-paced LMS.

| Capability | First commercial release | Subsequent depth |
|---|---|---|
| Learning delivery | Native activities, files/links, quizzes, assignments, live sessions and resume progress | Advanced adaptive sequences and rich simulations |
| Course authoring | Draft, review, publish, version, source attribution and accessibility checks | Collaborative expert review, controlled translation and reusable activity libraries |
| Pathways | Prerequisites, assignments, due dates, completion rules and reassignment | Equivalencies, exemptions and richer skills-based progression |
| Provider operation | Client workspaces, reusable offerings, public/private groups, capacity, enrolment and invoicing links | Resource optimisation, complex pricing, licensing and renewals |
| Enterprise operation | Scoped groups, manager views, programmes, records and exports | HR-driven lifecycle, multiple providers and deeper workforce planning |
| Trust and operations | Scoped access, audit history, backups, export and tested recovery | Contract-specific residency, retention, support and assurance requirements |

Keep breadth disciplined through customer evidence. An enterprise pilot may require SSO before launch; a provider may require imported course playback. Those are deal-specific gates, not features to defer automatically because they are less novel.

Support customers' existing tools. For packaged learning, evaluate a proven playback service rather than building a SCORM runtime first. For external learning tools, evaluate LTI where supported. For learning activity interchange, choose defined xAPI profiles and a tested LRS integration when required. These are separate interoperability needs, not interchangeable standards. Sources: [SCORM Cloud documentation](https://cloud.scorm.com/docs/user-guide/getting-started/), [1EdTech LTI](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/lti), [ADL guidance](https://www.adlnet.gov/guides/tla/service-definitions/xAPI-Adoption-TLA-CMM-Level-1.html).

Use integration adapters for HR identity, calendars, video meetings, payments, accounting and existing LMS records. Record source identifiers, sync status and field ownership. Externally reported completion should be labelled with its origin and assessment meaning.

## 9. Engineering principles for reliable agents

Keep the current application stack while adding a durable workflow execution layer and shared domain actions. This is not a reason to split immediately into many services.

The action path is: **request or event → scoped plan → policy check → approval if required → validated domain action → recorded result → verification**.

Agents and interface controls invoke the same operations. Permission checks occur server-side for each action. Model-supplied organisation IDs must not establish authority. Search and retrieval enforce the current content permissions, including revocations, before returning material.

Persist workflow progress so work survives restarts and can wait for a human decision or future date. Give external actions unique identifiers and reconcile uncertain results before retrying. If an invoice request times out after the billing system accepted it, retrying blindly must not create a second invoice.

Save approved plan scope and record versions. Recheck price, seat availability, permissions and source version at execution time. A stale approval requires re-planning where the material conditions changed. Use explicit compensating actions when a completed external action cannot be undone.

Treat uploaded training material and retrieved text as content, not authority to run operations. Separate external content from tool permissions. Keep authorisation, completion thresholds, billing arithmetic and seat allocation in deterministic code.

Evaluate against realistic tasks: partial records, conflicts, duplicate events, revoked access, malicious instructions in a document, stale approvals and failed integrations. Measure correctness and unwanted side effects as well as speed. Preserve a human recovery path with the actual underlying records visible.

## 10. Brand and messaging architecture

**Experrt** is the platform. **Experrt Academy** can identify your training business, if you adopt that naming. Other providers operate under their own brands. Platform customers should not land in an academy catalogue that appears to compete with them.

Proposed platform homepage copy, to use when the corresponding workflows ship:

**Your learning programmes, moving forward.**

Experrt helps training providers and L&D teams design, deliver and manage learning. Agents coordinate the work, while your people stay in control of teaching, decisions and records.

Actions: **For training providers** and **For enterprise L&D**. Secondary action: **See a programme in action**.

Provider page:

**Run more training with less coordination.**

Bring clients, courses, delivery and learner records into one workspace. Prepare programmes with AI, manage the exceptions, and give each client a clear view of progress under your brand.

Enterprise page:

**Turn learning priorities into programmes that get delivered.**

Coordinate internal experts and training partners, guide employees through practical learning, and keep an accountable record of progress.

Academy page:

**Build the skills to put AI and robotics to work.**

Practical programmes delivered by Experrt, with guided learning, workplace practice and expert feedback.

Agent task copy should describe work and state: Draft programme ready; Two scheduling conflicts need a decision; Three approved reminders sent; Report awaiting missing assessment results. Avoid a generic “AI is thinking” indicator for a workflow that has actually stopped on missing data.

Until these workflows exist, label product demonstrations as previews. Use the current truthful training and records copy for live services. Do not market the proposed platform as operational merely because the architecture has been specified.

## 11. Commercial strategy

Your academy earns training revenue. The platform earns software revenue from providers and enterprises. Keep separate cost centres so discounted internal use does not hide platform delivery costs.

Provider pricing hypothesis: a workspace subscription covering administration and a defined learner/activity allowance, with additional client portals or capacity where they drive cost. Include a predictable agent allowance. Enterprise pricing hypothesis: an annual platform contract based on agreed scale, integration and support needs, with defined usage limits.

Both need paid implementation where data migration or integrations create substantial work. Validate pricing with buyers and cost data; do not set it from competitor price lists alone. Avoid charging per agent action in the primary offer, which makes automation feel unpredictable. Preserve transparent limits and budget visibility.

The previous free-platform positioning does not automatically fit selling software to training companies. Reconcile the new offer explicitly and honour existing commitments. A client viewer portal can be included in a provider's contract without granting the full enterprise operating suite free of charge.

Sell enterprises through two routes: a complete platform where replacement is realistic, and a connected programme workspace where an incumbent LMS remains authoritative for existing records. Start with the route that avoids a forced migration before value has been demonstrated.

## 12. Rollout grounded in your own business

### Stage 1: run your next real programme

Map one actual client engagement from enquiry through reporting. Capture present admin time, duplicate entry, missing information and support requests. Build the programme workspace, client relationship, basic learning activities and record trail around that engagement. Use your real operating role, not platform administrator privileges.

First agents: prepare a programme from approved material, and reconcile delivery tasks against the schedule. Both should produce inspectable records and exception queues.

Exit: the programme can be delivered without spreadsheet reconciliation being essential to establish attendance, results or client reporting. Any remaining manual steps are visible and intentionally supported.

### Stage 2: prove independence with another provider

Onboard an external provider with its own brand, content, clients and reporting requirements. Test ordinary setup, data import, client isolation, support access and billing. No provider-specific code fork.

Exit: the provider can operate an engagement independently and export its records. Your academy has no routine visibility into its private business.

### Stage 3: prove enterprise operation

Choose a bounded department or programme with an L&D sponsor. Add the required identity/integration controls and connect internal learning with external delivery. Establish source ownership before syncing.

Exit: L&D can plan, assign, monitor and report without the provider controlling the enterprise's entire learning operation. Records can be reconciled with the incumbent system when one exists.

### Stage 4: expand proven automation

Extend standing permissions for reliable actions, introduce expert-reviewed content maintenance and expand practical AI/robotics evidence. Build resource optimisation and ecosystem integrations when repeated customer demand justifies them.

Treat these as evidence gates. The earlier twelve-week outline covers a focused pilot, not a mature LMS for two markets. A credible delivery estimate needs staffing, imported-content requirements, current programme volume and enterprise integration commitments.

## 13. Measures that determine whether it works

Provider outcomes: administrative time per delivered programme, avoidable schedule changes, data re-entry, report preparation time, programme margin and customer renewal.

Enterprise outcomes: time from approved need to assigned programme, completion of reviewed practical work, coverage against declared role requirements, overdue reviews and L&D coordination time.

Learner outcomes: useful first action, successful practice, quality of feedback, progress on comparable assessments and retention at an agreed follow-up point.

Agent outcomes: completed authorised tasks, corrections required, duplicate or unintended effects, exceptions resolved, cost per successful task and human time saved on a measured baseline. Click reduction alone is insufficient.

Record outcomes: completeness, correction frequency, source reconciliation and tested access boundaries. These make the automation credible.

## The immediate product brief

Build **one programme workspace that your company can use to deliver training to one enterprise client**. Include reusable learning content, a scoped client portal, learner activities, facilitator review, durable records and two agents that prepare and coordinate the programme.

That first slice exercises the provider, enterprise and learner relationships together. It gives the platform a real operational centre, while leaving room to become a broader LMS through measured expansion.
