# Experrt reviewer calibration and decision record
Read the course-specific expected results as well as the common assessment guide. Evaluate the learner's files and results, not the appearance of the report or the practice percentage. Require independent explanations of the consequential decisions.

## Review sequence
1. Identify learner, course version, attempt, evidence locations and whether the demonstration is live or simulated.
2. Inspect brief, actual permissions and source snapshots before assessing the output.
3. Check one final claim or behaviour independently and inspect an exception test.
4. Replay or inspect the practice problem and check actual effects for duplicates.
5. Ask the course-specific changed-case question. Record the learner's explanation and any assistance given.
6. Score each dimension separately, with an evidence reference and improvement action. A score below 3 in any dimension returns the attempt for revision. Do not compensate through averaging.
7. Record the decision and require a verified certificate workflow after a pass; never issue from browser practice progress.

## Calibration examples
- Attractive output, but no source or test evidence: verification 0 or 1 depending on what can actually be inspected; return the work.
- Correct normal output, but a missing field is silently guessed and needs prompting to fix: verification 2; require an independently explained missing-data case and retest.
- Complete permission table, but an external message is sent without required approval: authority 1; investigate the actual effect, restore the boundary and require a fresh demonstration.
- Correct checks, tested boundaries and recovery, with independent explanation: score 3 where the evidence meets each criterion.
- Same competent evidence plus a tested improvement on unseen cases with measured cost and documented limits: score 4 for the relevant dimension only.

## Feedback format
Skill: [name]. Score: [0–4]. Evidence: [file/run/case]. Observed: [specific behaviour]. Required improvement: [concrete action]. Reassessment: [case or result to inspect].

## Consistency check
For the AI assessment service, calibrate missing evidence, unsafe behaviour, conflicting instructions and a complete example against this guide. Record model, policy, scores and limits. Live platform demonstrations and learner pilots must be recorded separately; automated checks do not establish those results.

# Course-specific evidence checks

## 1. Decide who you are writing for and how you want to sound

The brief defines reader purpose, approved claims, exclusions and a helpful next step.

The agent makes an unsupported qualification promise to strengthen the workshop advert. How should the draft be handled?

Best response: Correct the claims without supporting facts and have the exact revised draft reviewed.
The approved fact pack establishes what can be claimed. Material corrections change the draft and require a review of that revision.

Alternative: Keep the stronger promise because it makes the message more persuasive.
A persuasive claim still needs support. Remove invented qualifications, benefits or prices.

Alternative: Publish first and correct the facts if readers notice.
This transfers fact-checking to readers and breaches the publishing boundary. Complete review before the external action.

## 2. Check the facts before you write

Every published-style claim has an approved fact ID or is removed.

The draft states £30 and same-day collection while the approved pack says otherwise. Which check should establish the correction?

Alternative: Use the newest article without checking its original source.
A new article can repeat old or unsupported information. Inspect the underlying evidence and its event date.

Alternative: Treat several repeats of the same announcement as separate confirmation.
Repeated reporting shares the same evidential origin. Record that dependence rather than inflating confidence.

Best response: Check the original record, its scope and the date the event takes effect.
This establishes what the source supports and when it applies. State any inference separately from the verified fact.

## 3. Write your drafts and choose helpful images

The batch uses supported facts and one accessible asset with an origin record.

Two images are available, but one has no known origin or usage conditions. Which asset decision is appropriate?

Alternative: Omit every image to avoid maintaining an asset record.
The course requires an accessible visual. Record the origin of an appropriate asset rather than removing the learning aid.

Best response: Use an asset with a recorded origin and established usage conditions, with useful alternative text.
A usable image needs both an appropriate visual purpose and a defensible source record. Check it at the intended display size.

Alternative: Use the unidentified photo because it is publicly visible online.
Public visibility does not establish permission to reuse. Confirm rights or choose a properly documented alternative.

## 4. Decide who checks and approves each draft

Each draft has a clear state and version; no send or publish effect occurs.

The webpage draft is reviewed, but the agent also wants to email it to subscribers. What authority applies?

Alternative: Proceed because the connected application technically permits the action.
Technical access is not authorisation. Enforce the agreed rule about what the agent may do and check that no external effect occurred.

Alternative: Treat an earlier approval for another draft as permission for this action.
Approval belongs to particular content and circumstances. A new destination or revision needs a fresh decision under the stated policy.

Best response: Prepare the draft and request approval for the specific action that affects other people or records.
The instruction permits preparation but reserves the external effect for approval. Show the current content, destination and action to the owner.

## 5. Correct the content and avoid publishing it twice

All affected formats are updated and tied to a new review; no duplicate publication is recorded.

The price changes after revision 1 was approved. Which action preserves a trustworthy editorial process?

Alternative: Change only the first draft the reviewer is likely to read.
Unchanged sibling outputs would remain inaccurate. Use the content inventory to identify every affected format.

Best response: Update all affected outputs, record the new version and obtain review of that version.
A changed fact can affect several formats. Trace the change through the batch and tie approval to the current content.

Alternative: Reuse the old approval because the overall topic has not changed.
The approval applied to earlier facts and content. The new version needs the review required by the workflow.

## 6. Finish your content and check how much time it took

The final batch is fact-checked, accessible, revision-controlled and supported by a qualified value comparison.

The agent drafts quickly but fact-checking takes longer than before. Which measure should guide the business decision?

Alternative: Compare only the agent’s execution time with all manual work.
This excludes human work required to obtain an acceptable result and overstates the improvement.

Alternative: Convert all saved time into guaranteed cash savings.
Time has value but does not automatically remove a cash cost. Label the assumed rate and explain whether any expenditure actually changes.

Best response: Include preparation, review, correction, maintenance and operating charges.
Value depends on the whole process at an equivalent quality standard. Separate one-off setup from time spent on the regular task.
