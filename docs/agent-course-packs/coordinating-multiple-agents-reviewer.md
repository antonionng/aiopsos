# Experrt reviewer calibration and decision record
Read the course-specific expected results as well as the common assessment guide. Evaluate the learner's files and results, not the appearance of the report or the practice percentage. Require independent explanations of the consequential decisions.

## Review sequence
1. Identify learner, course version, attempt, evidence locations and whether the demonstration is live or simulated.
2. Inspect brief, actual permissions and source snapshots before assessing the output.
3. Check one final claim or behaviour independently and inspect an exception test.
4. Replay or inspect the practice problem and check actual effects for duplicates.
5. Ask the course-specific changed-case question. Record the learner's explanation and any assistance given.
6. Score each dimension separately, with an evidence reference and improvement action. A score below 3 in any dimension returns the attempt for revision. Do not compensate through averaging.
7. Record the decision and require a verified certificate workflow after a pass; never issue from browser practice progress.

## Calibration examples
- Attractive output, but no source or test evidence: verification 0 or 1 depending on what can actually be inspected; return the work.
- Correct normal output, but a missing field is silently guessed and needs prompting to fix: verification 2; require an independently explained missing-data case and retest.
- Complete permission table, but an external message is sent without required approval: authority 1; investigate the actual effect, restore the boundary and require a fresh demonstration.
- Correct checks, tested boundaries and recovery, with independent explanation: score 3 where the evidence meets each criterion.
- Same competent evidence plus a tested improvement on unseen cases with measured cost and documented limits: score 4 for the relevant dimension only.

## Feedback format
Skill: [name]. Score: [0–4]. Evidence: [file/run/case]. Observed: [specific behaviour]. Required improvement: [concrete action]. Reassessment: [case or result to inspect].

## Consistency check
For the AI assessment service, calibrate missing evidence, unsafe behaviour, conflicting instructions and a complete example against this guide. Record model, policy, scores and limits. Live platform demonstrations and learner pilots must be recorded separately; automated checks do not establish those results.

# Course-specific evidence checks

## 1. Decide whether you need more than one agent

The role split has an evidence-based justification and does not treat speed as a substitute for correctness.

The three-agent run is faster but still fails the claim standard. How should it compare with the original process?

Best response: Compare both workflows on the same cases and quality standard before choosing.
Coordination adds costs and may still produce claims without supporting facts. Use equivalent cases to assess whether the role split helps.

Alternative: Choose the larger team because specialised roles guarantee better quality.
Role names do not establish independence or accuracy. Test the team against original sources.

Alternative: Choose whichever workflow finishes its automated steps first.
Elapsed execution omits review and corrections. Compare the complete process and acceptance failures.

## 2. Give each agent a clear job and the information it needs

Responsibilities and authority do not overlap ambiguously; one human owns the final decision.

The drafter and reviewer agree they can publish without asking the owner, despite a draft-only brief. What authority applies?

Alternative: Proceed because the connected application technically permits the action.
Technical access is not authorisation. Enforce the agreed rule about what the agent may do and check that no external effect occurred.

Alternative: Treat an earlier approval for another draft as permission for this action.
Approval belongs to particular content and circumstances. A new destination or revision needs a fresh decision under the stated policy.

Best response: Prepare the draft and request approval for the specific action that affects other people or records.
The instruction permits preparation but reserves the external effect for approval. Show the current content, destination and action to the owner.

## 3. Decide how agents pass work to each other

The receiving role rejects missing evidence and obsolete brief versions before drafting.

The researcher’s handoff lacks a source ID for its strongest claim. How should the drafter proceed?

Alternative: Allow the agents to approve each other’s expanded authority.
Agreement between agents does not change the owner’s permissions. Keep the final decision with the assigned human owner.

Best response: Reject the incomplete handoff, name the missing evidence and retain the accountable owner.
The receiving role needs the agreed requirements for the information received. Record the rejection rather than silently guessing a source or authority.

Alternative: Fill the missing source with a plausible reference so work can continue.
A plausible reference may not support the claim. Return the handoff with the specific gap.

## 4. Run the agents together and check their work

The final draft contains only supported claims, with an available to check review decision.

The reviewer trusts the drafter’s added claim without opening its source. Which evidence standard should apply?

Alternative: Use the newest article without checking its original source.
A new article can repeat old or unsupported information. Inspect the underlying evidence and its event date.

Alternative: Treat several repeats of the same announcement as separate confirmation.
Repeated reporting shares the same evidential origin. Record that dependence rather than inflating confidence.

Best response: Check the original record, its scope and the date the event takes effect.
This establishes what the source supports and when it applies. State any inference separately from the verified fact.

## 5. Deal with disagreements and work that gets stuck

The loop ends at the stated limit and work resumes only after the conflict is resolved.

Drafter and reviewer are about to begin a third unchanged revision cycle. What should happen?

Alternative: Choose the most confident agent without examining the sources.
Confidence is not an evidential tie-breaker. Compare the underlying records and escalate unresolved judgement.

Best response: Stop at the revision limit and give the owner the conflicting evidence.
A limit on revisions keeps costs under control and shows when a person needs to decide. Keep both views and the facts behind them.

Alternative: Continue exchanging drafts until every agent agrees.
Repeated agreement-seeking can spend indefinitely without new evidence. Honour the revision limit.

## 6. Compare the team with using one agent

The project demonstrates handoff validation, independent review, recovery with clear limits and measured value.

The team produces a polished final document but no handoff or conflict records. What is missing from practical validation?

Alternative: Award a certificate because every practice activity is marked complete.
Practice progress shows which activities you have worked through. A certificate needs a pass in the practical assessment.

Alternative: Use high scores in other areas to make up for the failed permission check.
You need at least 3 out of 4 in every area. If the permission check is below that score, improve that part and have it checked again.

Best response: Have a reviewer check the work and provide anything still missing.
The reviewer needs to check your skills in every required area. Finishing practice activities and producing a good-looking result are not enough to show that you have passed.

# Individual changed-case challenge
Give the drafter a handoff with a missing source and an obsolete brief version. Show that the handoff is rejected before drafting and that the final owner sees the unresolved gap.
