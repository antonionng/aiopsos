# Course introduction video instructions

Create one narrated, illustrated course introduction for the course in the selected source. Target 3 to 4 minutes. This is a public course-page overview for an adult beginner, not a condensed teaching lesson, interview, podcast or advert promising career outcomes.

Use a warm trainer voice and British English. Speak directly to the learner in natural, connected sentences. Pronounce Experrt as “expert”, Northstar as “north star”, and AI as “ay eye” (never say “A and I”). Explain unfamiliar terms when first used. Do not refer to “the source”, “this document”, “our research” or imaginary studies. Do not use hype, scolding, childish metaphors, salary statistics, invented testimonials or absolute guarantees.

Explain: who this specific course is for; the familiar work problem it addresses; what its actual lessons cover; what learners will produce; the correct assessment and certificate process; how the practical skills can help in their current work and provide an example for a career conversation; price and access only as stated; and the next step of reading the outline and trying the sample or buying if it fits. Treat the course-specific learning and assessment facts as constraints. An agent course's AI project assessment must never be applied to a self-paced course with scenario questions and a signed final document.

Keep career benefits grounded in demonstrated work. Do not say the course guarantees employment, a promotion, higher pay, accreditation, legal compliance or fixed time savings. Do not add platform capabilities, included subscriptions, support services or measured business results. Keep fictional organisations explicitly fictional. Do not imply that watching this introduction completes the course or earns the certificate.

End with a calm invitation: “Read the course outline below and decide whether this is the right next step for you.” Keep commercial detail brief; the page carries the full terms. No exercise or assessment is required during this introduction.

## Custom visual style

Experrt Academy: warm cream #FFFCF7, deep plum #2D2439, purple #7044B2 and pale lavender #EEE7FA. Use editorial illustrations of adult professionals, notebooks, files and practical work. Consistent purple and cream slides, generous space, large readable headings, and simple labelled diagrams. Use the course title, actual module names and actual final project. Show at most three short ideas per slide. Avoid neon green, bright blue, stock-business photography, busy dashboards and fake performance charts. Keep Google’s watermark.

## Additional constraints following the first recording review

Use calm, descriptive trainer language. Avoid filler such as “boom”, “absolutely”, “conquer”, “undeniable” or “master everything”. Describe practice as practice. A submitted project or signed document is an example of learning, not independent proof of workplace competence. Never imply unlimited assessment attempts. For agent courses there are up to 20 completed AI assessments per purchase and no more than three in a 24-hour period. Self-paced team purchases support two to fifty places, never sixty. Include only these numbers if relevant to this specific source. Do not substitute a generic course or module title. End with a useful invitation to read the outline, without pressure or promises. Keep the requested purple and cream illustrations consistent throughout.

Explain this course’s assessment positively. Instructions about other course types are production constraints, not lines to narrate. For example, do not say an agent course is “not a self-paced scenario with signed final documents”; describe its actual project, feedback and certificate instead. Prefer “check” and “clear instructions” to repeated phrases such as “rigorous oversight” and “crystal-clear configuration”.
