# Managing Agents Reliably | Course introduction

Course version: 2026.10.2-ai-assessed. This is a public introduction to this specific course, not a lesson or a promise of career outcomes.

## Who the course is for and what it helps you do

Welcome to Experrt Academy. Anyone responsible for agents that continue working between conversations. In this course, you will learn to learn how to check an agent’s work, spot problems and restart a failed task without doing the same work twice.

An AI agent is software that works through an agreed task using the information and tools it is allowed to access. Its actual capabilities depend on the product, account and setup you use. You remain responsible for setting limits and checking results. Do not claim that a product has been independently tested on the learner’s account.

## What the six modules cover

1. Decide who is responsible and write down the task. The practical work you keep is: Responsibility inventory and accountable owner map.
2. Choose what your agent can see and do. The practical work you keep is: Table of allowed actions and boundary challenge results.
3. Make a set of examples to test your agent. The practical work you keep is: Success, ambiguity and problem-situation set of checks.
4. Fix a failed task without doing the same work twice. The practical work you keep is: Incident timeline and a tested recovery procedure.
5. Track spending, checking time and useful alerts. The practical work you keep is: Cost model and notification tuning comparison.
6. Check that the task works and explain it to someone else. The practical work you keep is: Audit findings, corrected workflow and project explanation.

## How you learn and what you will produce

You study at your own pace using illustrated explanations, worked examples, practice questions with feedback and practical exercises. Your account saves your notes. Your final practical project: Audit and improve a flawed workflow, then demonstrate permission checks, verification, incident recovery and measurable value. Keep the instructions, relevant settings, results and checks that explain what happened. Where a product step cannot be completed, label your work as a practice simulation and say what still needs testing in the actual product.
