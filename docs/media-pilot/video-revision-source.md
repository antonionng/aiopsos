# Experrt Academy: Choose a task you do every day or week

This is an AI-generated pilot for module 1 of Always-On Agents: Foundations. The lesson is for beginners. You will choose a regular task, describe what a correct result should include and record how long the task takes today. You can complete the exercise on paper, using your own work or the fictional Northstar Studio example.

## Choose something familiar

An AI agent is software that can carry out agreed steps using the information and tools you give it. An always-on agent can continue agreed work between your conversations with it. Tools differ: some depend on particular apps or computers being available. You still need to explain the task, provide its information and check the result.

Start with something you do every day or week and already understand, such as preparing a report, checking deadlines or sorting new requests. When you know how to do the task yourself, you can recognise a mistake in the draft. “Manage my business” leaves too many decisions unexplained. “Prepare a draft of this week's project report from these records” gives you a specific result to check.

## Practise with Northstar Studio

Northstar Studio is a fictional six-person design business. Its owner wants a weekly report showing which projects need attention. The studio's project tracker is a list of projects, their progress and their deadlines. The practice tracker contains five records.

For this exercise, the agent may read the supplied tracker and prepare a draft report. The owner checks the draft and decides what happens next. The agent has no permission to contact customers, change records or change prices. Keeping the result as a draft gives the owner an opportunity to check it; the owner still needs to carry out that check.

Compare report preparation with chasing customer payments and choosing prices. A report draft can be checked and corrected before it is shared. A payment message has already reached a customer once it is sent. Changing a price can affect what someone is charged. Those actions need separate decisions and permission. For Northstar's first practice task, choose preparation of the report and keep customer contact, prices and record changes with the owner.

## Ask three questions

First, do you have the information the task needs? Northstar's report needs the supplied tracker. If that file is missing, the agent should report the problem to the simulated owner rather than invent a current report.

Second, can you check the result? Decide what a correct draft should include. Northstar's report needs a reporting date and project IDs, with overdue work, blocked work and missing information listed separately. A project ID is a short label that helps you find the original record behind a statement. Check the draft against those records, rather than judging whether it sounds confident.

Third, can you correct a mistake before anyone relies on the work? For your first attempt, choose a draft task and keep sending messages or changing records separate. Name the person who will check the draft. This person is the reviewer: the person responsible for checking the work.

## Check the example against its records

Northstar's fictional reporting date is 1 October 2026 at 09:00 Europe/London. In this example, overdue means a project is not complete and its due date is earlier than the reporting date.

P101, the Harbour brochure, is open and was due on 25 September, so it is overdue. P102, the Elm identity project, is complete, so it is not overdue even though its due date has passed. P103, the Larch website, is open with a missing due date. List that missing information separately; it does not prove the project is overdue. The point is to choose work where you can explain the result using the source information.

## Record your starting point

Write down how long the task takes today, including preparation, checking and fixing mistakes. This starting record is sometimes called a baseline. It gives you something to compare with later. When you try the agent, compare at least three attempts with your usual method, using the same standard of quality. Include your own checking and correction time. A faster draft may still take longer overall if it needs many corrections. Use your records to decide whether the approach helps; no particular time saving is promised.

## Complete the exercise

Write down three tasks you do every day or week. For each one, explain the information you use, who needs the result and how you check it. Use the three questions to choose one task where the agent can prepare a draft for review.

Write down why you chose it, who checks it, what a correct result should include and which decisions you keep. Record how long it takes today, including preparation, checking and corrections. Your finished work should contain the three possible tasks, your chosen task and reasons, the checking person, your description of a correct result and your timing record.

If you prefer to use Northstar, compare report preparation, chasing payments and choosing prices. Choose the report draft and explain why it can be checked before it affects anyone. Describe how you would measure the time; do not present imagined timings as measurements.

Pause here and complete the exercise. Then read your choice back: could another person understand the task, its information, who checks it and what a correct result includes? Make anything unclear more specific. This is a practice lesson from one module; it does not complete the whole course or award a certificate.
