## Instructions to paste into the generation dialogue

Create an 8 to 12 minute two-host learning discussion for Experrt Academy, using only the supplied module 1 source, “Choose a task you do every day or week”. One host is a clear, patient trainer; the other asks practical questions a beginner would ask. Use natural British English, complete explanations and a friendly adult tone. Avoid hype, exaggerated agreement, repeated banter, slogans and unexplained jargon. Pronounce Experrt like “expert”, Northstar as “North star”, and dates in British order. Identify this as an AI-generated pilot from Experrt Academy near the start.

Explain what the learner will practise and produce, and define an AI agent in simple language. Discuss why a familiar everyday or weekly task helps you check the work. Explain that always-on tools differ and some depend on particular apps or computers being available. Introduce Northstar Studio as a fictional six-person design studio, and explain its project tracker before referring to it.

Let the beginner host ask why preparing a weekly report differs from chasing customer payments or choosing prices. The trainer should explain the difference between preparing a draft for review and taking actions that already affect other people. Northstar's agent only reads the supplied tracker and prepares a draft. The owner checks the draft and retains customer contact, record changes and pricing decisions.

Work through the three questions with clear examples: do you have the information, can you check the result, and can you correct a mistake before anyone relies on it? If discussing project records, accurately use the source's fixed reporting date of 1 October 2026. P101 is overdue; P102 is complete and not overdue; P103 has a missing due date which needs a separate flag. Do not invent facts, agent capabilities, statistics, timings, savings or project details. Explain why confident wording cannot replace checking against the original records.

Have the beginner ask “Does a faster draft mean I have saved time?” Explain the starting timing record, or baseline, and include preparation, checking and corrections. Later compare at least three attempts at the same quality standard, including checking time. Make no promise of savings.

Close by guiding the learner through the source's exercise: write three regular tasks; explain each task's information, reader and checks; choose one draft task; name the person checking it; describe a correct result and decisions you keep; record current preparation, checking and correction time. Offer the fictional Northstar comparison for people who do not want to use real work. Do not add an installation task, live account connection or other requirements. End with a clear invitation to pause, write their task choice and check whether another person could understand it. The sample is one module's introduction, not the whole course or a certificate assessment.
